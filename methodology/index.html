Methodology

A win-probability model built only from game data: results, schedules, box scores and official injury reports. Market prices are never inputs — they are only used afterwards, to compare.

The full math guide (PDF)

Pipeline

1. Team ratings (Elo)

Each team has a rating; the forecast for a game is P(home) = 1 / (1 + 10−d/400), whered is the rating gap plus adjustments. After each game both ratings move by K × (result − forecast), K = 10.

  • Home court +50 rating points when the home team is the favorite, +35 otherwise; 0 at neutral sites.
  • Back-to-backs −60 for a team playing on consecutive days, counted on the arena's local date.
  • Offseason ratings keep 75% of their distance from average (1500).
  • Older seasons count less when fitting: K × 0.6 per season back.
  • Playoff games do not change regular-season ratings (tested: letting them in made forecasts worse).

2. Player availability

Ratings describe a team at full strength. The availability layer asks who will actually play. Each player has a rating (a blend of Game Score and on-court plus-minus relative to teammates, shrunk toward replacement level for small samples). The team's expected lineup strength is

λ · 28 · Σ P(plays | status) · projected minutes / 48 · (rating − replacement)

P(plays | status) comes from the official injury report published at least 60 minutes before tip-off — for example, how often a "Questionable" player has actually played. λ = 0.9 and every other setting were fit on 2021-22 to 2023-24 only, then tested once on 2024-25 and 2025-26.

3. Margins

The expected point margin is derived from the win probability: margin ~ Normal(σ · Φ−1(p), σ), σ = 13.6 points. A separately fitted margin model was not better.

4. No look-ahead — the rules

  1. Every input carries the time it became known. A forecast may only use inputs known before it was made; the code refuses to run otherwise.
  2. Ratings are updated in tip-off order. A guard fails the build if a game is processed after a later one.
  3. Injury information comes only from reports stored with their publication time — never from who actually played.
  4. Walk-forward validation only: parameters are fit on seasons before the test season.
  5. Market prices are never model inputs.
  6. A change is adopted only if the paired bootstrap (2,000 resamples) 95% CI of the per-game Brier difference excludes zero, or the gain is at least 0.0085 — the smallest difference a season of games can reliably detect.

5. Scoring

Brier score = mean of (forecast − outcome)²; log loss = mean of −log(probability given to what happened). Both reward honest probabilities; log loss punishes confident misses harder. Calibration checks that 70% forecasts come true about 70% of the time.
